A place of strength and memory

More than a landmark.
A sign of enduring faith.

St. Nicholas Church in Ichnya, in Ukraine's Chernihiv region, is more than an architectural landmark. It is a symbol of steadfast faith and the spiritual renewal of the region.

For decades, the only surviving part of the once-grand church complex was its distinctive bell tower. Today, it once again calls worshippers to prayer as an active church of the Orthodox Church of Ukraine.

History · Ichnya

Through the
pages of history

From a wooden church in Korybutivshchyna to a bell tower that survived destruction.

1701— 1879

The beginnings

Foundations and
wooden churches

The first records of St. Nicholas Church date to 1701. The church was built at the expense of Vasyl Ivanovych Revtiukh, a resident of Nizhyn, and his wife. The site was chosen in Korybutivshchyna, beyond Ichnya's ancient earthen fortress.

The original building was made of wood. Over the centuries, it endured four major fires, each time rebuilt through the devotion of its parishioners.

01
1879— 1930s

Masonry grandeur

Brick, stone
and five domes

In 1879, the church underwent a major stone reconstruction. A large five-domed masonry church rose alongside a tall bell tower.

Built in the brick style, at the intersection of eclecticism and modernism, the church followed a diocesan design also used for cathedrals in the Sumy region, including those in Lebedyn and Trostianets.

02
Sovietera

Destruction · survival

The church destroyed.
The bell tower preserved.

Church furnishings, icons, and relics were seized or destroyed. The main five-domed structure was dismantled for building materials: bricks were used for the neighbouring fire station, and local residents took the rest.

The tall bell tower was preserved as a fire lookout tower, serving as a watch post for the city fire service. This saved it from complete demolition.

03

A return to prayer

A new era
of renewal

The surviving bell tower became a church again, and the community continues restoring the sanctuary.

20072026

After Ukraine regained independence, efforts began to return the sanctuary to worshippers. The surviving bell tower was consecrated and began serving as an independent church.

In 2007, the parish officially joined the jurisdiction of the Orthodox Church of Ukraine, then known as the Ukrainian Orthodox Church of the Kyiv Patriarchate.

A NEW CHAPTER RAISING AND CONSECRATING THE NEW DOME · 2026

September 2026 · A historic event

2026

A new dome
above the church.

A new 14-metre dome topped with a cross was ceremonially raised and consecrated above St. Nicholas Church. This is the first and most important step towards restoring the historic appearance of this architectural treasure.
